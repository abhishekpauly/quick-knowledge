## What went well

- **The tour engine + advance-on model held up under real users.** Nobody got stuck on a "click Next" step; the click-driven advances felt natural in feedback.
- **PostHog as the confirmed sink saved a swap window.** Zero adapter changes at launch.
- **CI gate caught two would-be regressions during the launch week PRs** — one lint slip, one coverage drop below 80% functions in the react package. Both fixed pre-merge.
- **The 5-user test surfaced exactly the class of issues it's designed for** — copy that reads flat, a targeting affordance the tour didn't name. Nothing structural, all fixable in a Saturday.

## What went badly

- **Analytics dashboard filter mismatched the event payload name** (`duration_ms` vs. `durationMs`). Caught in T-067 verification, not in unit tests — because the mismatch was on the dashboard side. Consider a schema-export step that publishes an event dictionary the dashboard authors read against.
- **Checklist widget default position collided with AI Platform's help chat launcher** on 1 of 5 users. The fix was on their side (remap help launcher), but a widget that assumes bottom-right corner is undefended when the host product does the same. Consider a `preferredCorner: [order]` config for v0.2.
- **Content rewrite loop was tighter than expected.** We had 4 rewrite items and no repeat-user-test — reasonable for smoke, but if any rewrite introduces its own confusion we won't know until real production analytics.

## Surprises

- **Onboarding completion at 64.2% is higher than we forecast** (target was 60% floor). The `create-project` → `user-menu` step is the biggest single-step drop (12 pts). Hypothesis: users open a project instead of continuing the tour and never come back. Worth an experiment in v0.2: skip the user-menu step for anyone who created a project mid-tour.
- **Zero support tickets tagged "tour" in the first 48h** — expected +5–15%. Could mean nobody's confused; could also mean users aren't recognising the widget as something support can help with.
- **The Vue adapter never got a real production tour this launch** — AI Platform's onboarding shipped from React only. The un-deferral was right (we'll need it), just didn't matter this week.
